# AGI Timelines

## TL;DR

AGI timelines are estimates of how soon AI will match or exceed human capability across most or all cognitive domains. In a 2026 stage debate, [[dario-amodei|Dario Amodei]] and [[demis-hassabis|Demis Hassabis]] agreed AI will be enormously powerful and disagreed mainly on *when*: Amodei expects the [[ai-self-improvement-loop|self-improvement loop]] to drive fast progress (a model doing most of what software engineers do within six-to-twelve months), while Hassabis stands by a roughly end-of-decade timeline and stresses that natural science resists fast automation [prov:src-2026-06-14-hassabis-amodei-day-after-agi#t00:01:12-00:02:44|direct|2026-06-14].

## Key Facts

- Amodei: a model may do most or all of what software engineers do end-to-end within six to twelve months, with code and AI research as the accelerants [prov:src-2026-06-14-hassabis-amodei-day-after-agi#t00:01:12-00:02:44|direct|2026-06-14] [epistemic:: tentative]
- Hassabis: stands by a roughly end-of-decade timeline; verifiable domains (code, maths) automate faster than natural science, where outputs must be tested experimentally [prov:src-2026-06-14-hassabis-amodei-day-after-agi#t00:03:00-00:04:15|direct|2026-06-14] [epistemic:: tentative]
- Both frame the disagreement as one of timescale, not direction — they largely agree on the eventual outcome [prov:src-2026-06-14-hassabis-amodei-day-after-agi#t00:15:44-00:17:30|direct|2026-06-14]
- A key open variable for the timeline is how fast the self-improvement loop closes, which neither treats as settled [prov:src-2026-06-14-hassabis-amodei-day-after-agi#t00:08:15-00:09:20|direct|2026-06-14]

## Detail

The two positions differ on mechanism as much as on date. Amodei's faster timeline rests on the [[ai-self-improvement-loop|self-improvement loop]]: models good at coding and AI research produce the next generation and compress the development cycle, so code and research are the key accelerants — tempered by physical bottlenecks (chip manufacture, training time) that AI cannot speed up [prov:src-2026-06-14-hassabis-amodei-day-after-agi#t00:01:12-00:02:44|direct|2026-06-14].

Hassabis's caution rests on verifiability. Coding and mathematics give a checkable output, so they automate fast; natural science does not — a chemical-compound or physics prediction must be tested experimentally, slowing the loop — and the hardest, possibly-missing, capability is generating the hypothesis or question in the first place [prov:src-2026-06-14-hassabis-amodei-day-after-agi#t00:03:00-00:04:15|direct|2026-06-14].

Both connect the timeline to downstream effects: Amodei ties his one-to-five-year labor-displacement horizon back to the same timeline logic, noting that capability-better-than-humans-at-everything in one-to-two years does not line up cleanly with displacement because of lag and replacement dynamics [prov:src-2026-06-14-hassabis-amodei-day-after-agi#t00:15:44-00:17:30|direct|2026-06-14].
