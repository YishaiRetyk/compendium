# Jagged Frontier

## TL;DR

A capability map for generative AI in which the edge of competence is not a smooth curve but a *jagged* line: AI nails certain tasks and fails on others that look comparably difficult, sometimes within the same conversation. [[hack-agentive-stack|Hack (Agentive Stack)]] argues that knowing where those edges sit — what the model nails, what it quietly gets wrong — is now part of the new developer literacy [prov:src-2026-05-03-is-this-the-only-skill-left#t00:04:56-00:05:21|direct|2026-05-04].

## Key Facts

- Coined in a 2023 working paper, "Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier" by Dell'Acqua, Kruse, Lifshitz-Assaf, Mollick et al. (Harvard Business School and collaborators) [epistemic:: inferred]
- Hack attributes the term to "Harvard researchers" without citing the specific authors or paper [prov:src-2026-05-03-is-this-the-only-skill-left#t00:04:56-00:05:01|direct|2026-05-04]
- The transcript renders the term as "jacked frontier" (likely speech-to-text artifact); the established term is "jagged frontier"
- Edges shift between sessions and even within a session, so static heuristics about "what AI can do" decay quickly [prov:src-2026-05-03-is-this-the-only-skill-left#t00:05:01-00:05:21|direct|2026-05-04]

## Detail

### The shape of the frontier

Traditional capability arguments imagine AI advancing along a smooth boundary: tasks below the line are automated, tasks above are not. The jagged frontier framing replaces that with a fractal-edged region: the model is sharp in some areas and surprisingly dull in others, with no clean rule for predicting which is which [prov:src-2026-05-03-is-this-the-only-skill-left#t00:04:56-00:05:21|direct|2026-05-04].

### Practical consequence

Treating the frontier as smooth produces two kinds of error: (a) over-trusting the AI on tasks it quietly fails at, and (b) under-using it on tasks it would actually nail. The remedy Hack proposes is empirical: developers must learn the contour of the specific model they are using through deliberate testing, push-back, and review — not by reading capability claims [prov:src-2026-05-03-is-this-the-only-skill-left#t00:05:14-00:05:21|direct|2026-05-04].

### Relationship to systems thinking

Knowing where the edges sit is operationally close to the [[systems-thinking|Systems Thinking]] question "what breaks if I delete this?" — both are diagnostic skills that resist being delegated to the AI itself, because the AI is the system being audited.
