```markdown
### Inline Epistemic Markers

Per-claim epistemic status uses Dataview inline field syntax. This is SEPARATE from the provenance syntax and serves a different purpose.

**Syntax:**

```
[epistemic:: <status>]
```

**Valid statuses:**

| Status | Meaning | When to use |
|--------|---------|-------------|
| `sourced` | Claim directly from a source | Verbatim or close paraphrase with provenance |
| `inferred` | Synthesized from source content | Logical conclusion not explicitly stated |
| `tentative` | Weak evidence | Claim may not hold, flagged for review |
| `stale` | Likely outdated | Source has changed or claim is time-sensitive |

**Why Dataview syntax:** Inline fields (`[key:: value]`) are natively queryable by Dataview. This enables "find all tentative claims" queries that feed into the lint workflow (Section 11.3). Example query:

```dataview
TABLE file.name as Page
FROM "wiki"
FLATTEN file.lists.text as item
WHERE contains(item, "[epistemic:: tentative]")
```

### Page-Level vs Claim-Level Epistemic Status

- **Page-level:** The `epistemic_status` frontmatter field (Section 5) reflects the overall evidence quality of the page: `sourced`, `mixed`, `tentative`, or `stale`.
- **Claim-level:** Inline `[epistemic:: <status>]` markers apply to individual claims within a page. A page with `epistemic_status: mixed` contains claims with different confidence levels.

A page may have `epistemic_status: sourced` in frontmatter while containing individual `[epistemic:: inferred]` claims if the majority of content is directly sourced. Use `mixed` when the page has a significant proportion of non-sourced claims.

### Mixed Inline Grammar (Intentional Design)

Two distinct inline syntaxes coexist in wiki page bodies. This is an intentional design choice -- do NOT normalize to a single syntax.

| Syntax | Purpose | Queryable by | Parseable by |
|--------|---------|--------------|--------------|
| `[prov:<source_id>#<locator>\|<support_type>]` | Traceability -- links claim to source passage | grep, scripts | grep, regex |
| `[epistemic:: <status>]` | Discovery -- marks claim confidence level | Dataview, grep | Dataview, grep |

**Why two syntaxes:** Provenance needs script/grep parseability for automated validation (lint workflow). Epistemic status needs Dataview queryability for interactive discovery in Obsidian. Different tools, different syntax. Collapsing them would sacrifice one capability.

**Combined inline pattern:**

```
Claim text. [prov:source_id#locator|support_type] [epistemic:: status]
```

**Example:**

```markdown
- Kahneman demonstrated that human judgment relies on heuristics [prov:src-2026-04-09-thinking-fast-and-slow-part1#sec:introduction|direct] [epistemic:: sourced]
- The anchoring effect may be stronger in financial decisions [prov:src-2026-04-09-thinking-fast-and-slow-part1#p42|inferred] [epistemic:: inferred]
- Some researchers question whether heuristics are always irrational [epistemic:: tentative]
```

Note: Not every claim needs both markers. Provenance is omitted when the claim has no specific source (e.g., a synthesis or editorial note). Epistemic status is recommended on all factual claims.
```
