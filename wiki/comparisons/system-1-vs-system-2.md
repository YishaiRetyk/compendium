## TL;DR

[[Daniel Kahneman]]'s dual-process framework distinguishes fast intuitive thinking (System 1) from slow deliberate thinking (System 2). System 1 is the source of [[Cognitive Biases]] -- its speed and automaticity make it efficient but prone to predictable errors. System 2 can override System 1 but is effortful and, in Kahneman's phrase, "lazy."

## Bottom Line

Most errors in judgment stem from System 1 operating unchecked. Awareness of the dual-process distinction is the first step toward better [[Decision Making]]: recognizing when a situation calls for System 2 engagement rather than relying on System 1 intuitions.

## Comparison Table

| Dimension | System 1 | System 2 |
|-----------|----------|----------|
| Speed | Fast, milliseconds | Slow, seconds to minutes |
| Effort | Effortless, automatic | Effortful, requires concentration |
| Awareness | Operates below conscious awareness | Consciously experienced |
| Control | Involuntary -- cannot be "turned off" | Voluntary -- can be engaged or withheld |
| Error profile | Systematic biases (anchoring, availability, representativeness) | Computational errors, laziness, depletion |
| Examples | Recognizing a face, reading a word, detecting anger in a voice | Doing mental arithmetic, parallel parking, filling out a tax form |
| Capacity | High throughput, parallel processing | Limited capacity, serial processing |
| Learning | Learns slowly through repeated exposure | Can learn from single instruction |

## Detailed Comparison

The dual-process framework is the central organizing metaphor of *Thinking, Fast and Slow*. Kahneman describes System 1 as the mind's automatic pilot -- it generates impressions, intuitions, and feelings that System 2 may endorse, adjust, or override. System 1 is responsible for the effortless cognition that allows people to navigate daily life without deliberation: reading facial expressions, understanding simple sentences, driving on an empty road. [prov:src-2026-04-09-thinking-fast-and-slow-part1#sec:two-systems|direct] [epistemic:: sourced]

System 2 is engaged when System 1 encounters something unexpected or when deliberate reasoning is required. It monitors the quality of System 1's output and can intervene -- but it does so selectively and with a bias toward accepting System 1's suggestions. Kahneman emphasizes that System 2 is "lazy": it tends to accept the first plausible answer rather than investing effort in verification. This laziness explains why intelligent people still fall prey to cognitive biases -- the issue is not capability but allocation of attention. [prov:src-2026-04-09-thinking-fast-and-slow-part1#sec:attention|direct] [epistemic:: sourced]

Drawing on the framework presented in the source, the practical implication is that debiasing strategies should not rely on individual vigilance alone. Because System 2 is a limited resource that depletes with use, institutional and structural interventions -- checklists, pre-mortems, adversarial collaboration -- are more reliable than simply telling people to "think harder." This synthesis extends beyond what the source states directly about any single intervention, connecting the dual-process model to broader decision-making practice.
