# Peter Naur

## TL;DR

Danish computer scientist (1928–2016). Co-author of the Algol 60 report (the "N" in BNF — Backus–Naur Form) and author of the influential 1985 paper [[Programming as Theory Building]], which is the conceptual anchor for several arguments about AI-assisted software development [prov:src-2026-05-03-is-this-the-only-skill-left#t00:00:47-00:00:55|direct|2026-05-04].

## Key Facts

- Nationality: Danish [prov:src-2026-05-03-is-this-the-only-skill-left#t00:00:47-00:00:54|direct|2026-05-04]
- Profession: computer scientist [prov:src-2026-05-03-is-this-the-only-skill-left#t00:00:47-00:00:54|direct|2026-05-04]
- Known for the 1985 paper *Programming as Theory Building* [prov:src-2026-05-03-is-this-the-only-skill-left#t00:00:47-00:00:55|direct|2026-05-04]
- Also widely known (outside this wiki's current sources) as a co-author of the Algol 60 specification and the namesake of Backus–Naur Form [epistemic:: inferred]

## Detail

The 1985 paper *Programming as Theory Building* is the contribution Hack draws on directly: programs are theories that live in programmers' heads, and code is just the shadow of those theories [prov:src-2026-05-03-is-this-the-only-skill-left#t00:00:55-00:01:21|direct|2026-05-04]. This argument has been picked up across the AI-coding discourse to explain why AI generation of code does not, by itself, transfer the program — the theory still has to be built by a human.

The transcript renders the name once as "Peter Nauer" (likely a speech-to-text artifact); the canonical spelling is **Peter Naur**.
