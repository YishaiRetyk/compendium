## TL;DR

Magazine-style overview of prospect theory, the decision-theoretic framework that [[Daniel Kahneman]] and Amos Tversky published in *Econometrica* in 1979 as an alternative to expected utility theory. The article covers the three core components -- reference dependence, loss aversion, and probability weighting -- along with downstream phenomena (endowment effect, status quo bias, disposition effect) and applied uses in finance, medicine, and public policy.

## Key Takeaways

- Prospect theory proposes that people evaluate outcomes as gains or losses relative to a psychological reference point, not by absolute magnitude. [prov:src-2026-04-10-kahneman-prospect-theory#sec:introduction|direct] [epistemic:: sourced]
- Losses are weighted roughly twice as heavily as equivalent gains, with empirical loss-aversion coefficients typically falling between 1.5 and 2.5 and a consensus midpoint around 2.0. [prov:src-2026-04-10-kahneman-prospect-theory#sec:loss-aversion|direct] [epistemic:: sourced]
- The prospect theory value function is concave over gains, convex over losses, and steeper on the loss side, reflecting both reference dependence and diminishing sensitivity. [prov:src-2026-04-10-kahneman-prospect-theory#sec:reference-points|direct] [epistemic:: sourced]
- Prospect theory uses an inverted-S-shaped probability weighting function: small probabilities are overweighted and moderate-to-large probabilities are underweighted. [prov:src-2026-04-10-kahneman-prospect-theory#sec:probability-weighting|direct] [epistemic:: sourced]
- Loss aversion is produced by System 1's automatic emotional reaction, which explains why deliberate System 2 knowledge of the theory does not train it away even in expert decision-makers. [prov:src-2026-04-10-kahneman-prospect-theory#sec:dual-process|direct] [epistemic:: sourced]

## Extracted Claims

- Prospect theory was first published by Kahneman and Tversky in the journal *Econometrica* in 1979 as a direct alternative to the expected utility model. [prov:src-2026-04-10-kahneman-prospect-theory#sec:introduction|direct] [epistemic:: sourced]
- Kahneman held academic appointments over his career at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem, the University of British Columbia, the University of California Berkeley, and Princeton University. [prov:src-2026-04-10-kahneman-prospect-theory#sec:introduction|direct] [epistemic:: sourced]
- The 2002 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences citation pointed at prospect theory as a framework rather than at any single experiment. [prov:src-2026-04-10-kahneman-prospect-theory#sec:introduction|direct] [epistemic:: sourced]
- Empirical loss-aversion coefficients typically fall between 1.5 and 2.5, with about 2.0 as the consensus midpoint. [prov:src-2026-04-10-kahneman-prospect-theory#sec:loss-aversion|direct] [epistemic:: sourced]
- The endowment effect -- people demanding more to give up an object than they would pay to acquire it -- is usually explained as a downstream consequence of loss aversion, because ownership recodes disposal as a loss. [prov:src-2026-04-10-kahneman-prospect-theory#sec:loss-aversion|direct] [epistemic:: sourced]
- Status quo bias is explained as a consequence of loss aversion: any change from the current state mixes gains and losses, and losses weigh more heavily, giving the default option a built-in advantage. [prov:src-2026-04-10-kahneman-prospect-theory#sec:loss-aversion|direct] [epistemic:: sourced]
- Reference dependence means outcomes are evaluated relative to a reference point that is typically the status quo but may also be an expectation, an aspiration, or a recent peak. [prov:src-2026-04-10-kahneman-prospect-theory#sec:reference-points|direct] [epistemic:: sourced]
- Diminishing sensitivity means each additional dollar gained or lost contributes less marginal impact than the one before it, for both gains and losses. [prov:src-2026-04-10-kahneman-prospect-theory#sec:reference-points|direct] [epistemic:: sourced]
- The resulting prospect theory value function is concave over gains, convex over losses, and steeper on the loss side than on the gain side. [prov:src-2026-04-10-kahneman-prospect-theory#sec:reference-points|direct] [epistemic:: sourced]
- People overweight small probabilities and underweight moderate-to-large probabilities; the overall shape is an inverted-S probability weighting function. [prov:src-2026-04-10-kahneman-prospect-theory#sec:probability-weighting|direct] [epistemic:: sourced]
- Overweighting of small probabilities is offered as part of the explanation for the popularity of state lotteries and of insurance products with negative expected value. [prov:src-2026-04-10-kahneman-prospect-theory#sec:probability-weighting|direct] [epistemic:: sourced]
- Loss-aversion flinches are characteristic of System 1 processing: fast, effortless, and difficult to override even when System 2 knows the theory. [prov:src-2026-04-10-kahneman-prospect-theory#sec:dual-process|direct] [epistemic:: sourced]
- In financial decision-making, prospect theory underpins the disposition effect -- investors are more willing to sell winning positions than losing ones because realizing a loss crystallizes a psychological cost that a paper loss does not. [prov:src-2026-04-10-kahneman-prospect-theory#sec:applications|direct] [epistemic:: sourced]
- In medical decision-making, a treatment described as having a ninety percent survival rate is chosen more often than the same treatment described as having a ten percent mortality rate, because the different reference points code the outcomes differently. [prov:src-2026-04-10-kahneman-prospect-theory#sec:applications|direct] [epistemic:: sourced]
- Nudge theory -- using default settings, framing choices, and simplified enrollment to guide decisions without restricting options -- draws directly on prospect theory's findings about reference points and loss aversion. [prov:src-2026-04-10-kahneman-prospect-theory#sec:applications|direct] [epistemic:: sourced]
